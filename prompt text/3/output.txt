Done — the card is the home page now, not a drawing of it.

It used to be assembled: the brain cropped out of the canvas, then the name,
the blurb and a rule set beside it on a blank navy field. Close to the page, but
a separate thing that had to be kept in step by hand, and it had already drifted
— the blurb was a condensed lede the page never said.

So it is a photograph instead. The script serves the site, opens the home page,
lets the brain draw and grow exactly as a visitor sees it, and shoots the hero
band at 1200x630 — at one device pixel to one CSS pixel, so nothing is scaled up
or down. Whatever the hero looks like is what the card shows, and the two cannot
come apart again.

Three things come out of the frame first. The demo bar and the site's own header,
because neither belongs in a link preview. And the Spin switch: it is a control,
and a still of it reads as a brain someone has turned off. The switch is still
clicked — that is what settles the model while the shutter is open — it is just
hidden before the shot rather than photographed.

The atlas credit stays in. The card is a use of the tractography and CC BY-SA 4.0
wants it attributed wherever it appears. That does leave "Drag to turn it" in a
picture nobody can drag. It is the page's own line and I left it rather than
start the card drifting from the page again on day one — say the word and I will
trim that clause in the capture and keep the attribution.

The card's address carries a new marker so the preview services refetch it rather
than serve the old one from cache, and its alt text now describes the page.
